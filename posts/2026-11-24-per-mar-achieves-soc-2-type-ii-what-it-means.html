Per Mar Achieves SOC 2 Type II: What It Means

By AI Security & Compliance Desk November 24, 2026

Source: Google News

In an era where digital interconnectedness touches every layer of enterprise operations, physical and electronic security providers face unprecedented scrutiny. Organizations can no longer assess a security vendor solely on response times or physical coverage; data protection, operational integrity, and strict internal controls are equally paramount. Highlighting this trend, Per Mar Security Services has officially earned its SOC 2 Type II designation, as reported by Security Info Watch.

For enterprise security leaders and compliance officers, this milestone underscores an essential reality: third-party risk management (TPRM) now demands verifiable, sustained operational proof across every service partner. While a Type I audit confirms that suitable controls were properly designed at a single point in time, a SOC 2 Type II attestation evaluates how effectively those controls function over an extended observation window, typically ranging from six to twelve months.

Understanding the SOC 2 Type II Distinction

Achieving a SOC 2 Type II attestation requires an independent CPA firm to scrutinize an organization’s controls mapped against the American Institute of CPAs (AICPA) Trust Services Criteria. For modern security service vendors, these criteria usually center on:

  • Security (Common Criteria): Protecting systems, infrastructure, and monitoring data against unauthorized physical and logical access.
  • Availability: Ensuring monitoring centers, dispatch networks, and client-facing interfaces remain operational and resilient against disruptions.
  • Confidentiality: Safeguarding proprietary client configurations, building schematics, incident logs, and sensitive customer data.
  • Processing Integrity: Validating that automated alarm handling, alerts, and reporting occur accurately and without unauthorized tampering.

By evaluating these operational controls over a sustained period, the Type II report offers compliance teams concrete evidence that policies are not merely theoretical blueprints, but actively enforced daily practices. This evidence reduces administrative friction during vendor due diligence and helps enterprise procurement teams meet internal governance thresholds.

Cross-Framework Overlaps: ISO 27001, HIPAA, and CMMC

For organizations operating across multiple regulated sectors, evaluating a partner’s SOC 2 Type II report yields tangible downstream efficiencies. A solid SOC 2 foundation directly mirrors requirements in several prominent frameworks:

  • ISO/IEC 27001: The control mechanisms examined in SOC 2—such as identity management, continuous audit logging, and risk assessment—map closely to Annex A security controls.
  • HIPAA Security Rule: Healthcare clients leveraging third-party monitoring services must verify administrative, physical, and technical safeguards. A SOC 2 Type II report serves as strong third-party assurance for Business Associate Agreement (BAA) compliance.
  • CMMC (Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification): Defense industrial base suppliers evaluating facility security vendors can leverage Type II audit findings to support assessments under CMMC access control and physical protection domains.

When security vendors demonstrate continuous adherence to these baseline controls, enterprise compliance teams spend less time issuing bespoke security questionnaires and more time addressing actual operational risks.

Actionable Steps for Third-Party Risk Teams

Whenever an existing or prospective service provider attains a new SOC 2 Type II designation, compliance and audit professionals should take structured steps to validate coverage:

  1. Inspect the Scope and Criteria: Check the auditor’s report to verify which Trust Services Criteria were assessed and confirm whether any critical business units or technologies were excluded from the boundary.
  2. Review the Observation Period: Ensure there are no significant audit gaps between historical reports and verify that the testing window aligns with your contract renewal cycle.
  3. Examine Complementary User Entity Controls (CUECs): Identify the specific responsibilities your organization must maintain to ensure end-to-end security, such as maintaining access list hygiene or securing local perimeter credentials.
  4. Assess Auditor Exceptions: Scrutinize the testing tables for any noted exceptions, and review management’s remediation responses to evaluate potential residual exposure.

As supply chain attacks and vendor vulnerabilities remain primary threat vectors, independent attestations will continue to serve as the benchmark for vendor trust.
